# FIDE

FIDE is the international governing body of chess. Its Charter describes it as a non-governmental, non-profit organisation, founded on 20 July 1924 in Paris and registered in Switzerland, with its seat and headquarters in Lausanne.[^s1] The name is the acronym of the French *Fédération Internationale des Échecs*; in September 2026 the General Assembly amended the Charter so that the French name stands alongside the English "International Chess Federation" in the official name and emblems, while English remains the working language.[^s4] Its motto, *Gens una sumus*, is usually rendered "we are one family".[^s1]

## What FIDE does

For a club player, FIDE matters mainly through four things it controls. It issues the Laws of Chess and the rules for organising competitions; it sets the conditions for and awards international titles and ratings, and claims exclusive rights over official ratings and rankings; and it holds the sole right to organise the Chess Olympiad and the World Chess Championships, including online and variant events.[^s1] The International Olympic Committee recognised FIDE as an International Sport Federation in 1999.[^s1] Among its aims the Charter lists spreading chess among all nations, youth education, women's participation, fair play and the inclusion of disabled players.[^s1]

## Membership and structure

Members are national federations, one per country, which must hold principal authority over chess there; new members must come from a country recognised by the UN or the IOC.[^s1] They are grouped into four continents: the African Chess Confederation, the Asian Chess Federation (Asia and Oceania), the Confederation of Chess for Americas and the European Chess Union, with zones below them.[^s1] FIDE counted 201 affiliated federations at its centenary in 2024,[^s6] and delegates of 196 federations voted in the 2026 presidential election.[^s3]

The General Assembly of member federations is the highest authority: it amends the Charter, approves the budget and elects the President and Deputy President on a joint ticket.[^s1] The Council, with fifteen voting members including eight Vice-Presidents and the four Continental Presidents, is the strategic body with executive and law-making powers; a Management Board runs daily business; and elected commissions, among them the Ethics and Disciplinary Commission, are independent.[^s1][^s2] This structure came from the Charter that replaced the old Statutes on 1 March 2020, which also introduced a two-term limit for the President and a no-confidence procedure.[^s2] Terms are generally four years.[^s1]

## History

France proposed the federation, and Pierre Vincent of the French federation is regarded as its founder; the meeting was held during the 1924 Paris tournament, with fourteen or fifteen signatory countries.[^s5] The body was first called "FIE", and the abbreviation FIDE was adopted at the 1925 Zurich congress at Italy's request.[^s5] Under its first President, Alexander Rueb (1924–1949), FIDE recognised Capablanca as world champion in 1925, set up the International Master title in 1935, and after Alekhine's death in 1946 approved the 1948 match-tournament that brought the world championship under its control.[^s5][^s6] The Grandmaster title was first awarded in 1950.[^s5] FIDE has organised the Chess Olympiad since 1927.[^s6]

As of November 2026 FIDE has had eight Presidents: Rueb, Folke Rogard (1949–1970), Max Euwe (1970–1978), Friðrik Ólafsson (1978–1982), Florencio Campomanes (1982–1995), Kirsan Ilyumzhinov (1995–2018), Arkady Dvorkovich (2018–2026) and Timur Turlov.[^s3][^s6] Turlov was elected on 26 September 2026 at the General Assembly held during the 46th Olympiad in Samarkand, winning the second round with 110 votes; Viswanathan Anand was elected Deputy President.[^s3][^s4] The same Assembly approved a FIDE rating for Chess960, to be introduced by 1 June 2027.[^s4]

## Pitfalls

Two common confusions are worth avoiding. FIDE is a federation of national federations, not a replacement for them: a body such as US Chess keeps principal authority over chess in its own country, while FIDE handles international titles, ratings and championships.[^s1] And a FIDE news release only reports a decision; the binding text is the Charter and the regulations beneath it, such as the Laws of Chess, which the Charter places in a formal hierarchy of rules.[^s1][^s4]

## Connections

FIDE is one of the **chess organizations** and contains continental bodies such as the **European Chess Union**.[^s1] It issues the **FIDE Laws of Chess**, maintains the international **Elo rating** list and awards titles including the **grandmaster title**; it organises the **Chess Olympiad** and the **World Chess Championship**, which it took over in 1948.[^s1][^s5][^s6]
