# World Federation for Chess Composition

The World Federation for Chess Composition (WFCC) is the worldwide organisation for the composed side of chess: problems, endgame studies and the solving of them. Where FIDE governs over-the-board play, the WFCC sets the rules of composition, runs the official composing and solving competitions, publishes anthologies of the best works and awards the titles for composers, judges and solvers.[^s2] It began life inside FIDE and has been an independent body cooperating with FIDE since 2010.[^s5]

## From FIDE commission to federation

At its 1954 congress FIDE decided to create a permanent subcommission for problems and endgame studies. Its constituent meeting took place in Budapest on 15–17 May 1956, with G. Neukomm of Hungary as first president.[^s1] That founding meeting already set out the main tasks the body still has: an individual world championship in composition, a code of rules (later the Codex) and a regular anthology. At the 1957 meeting in Vienna the members agreed to publish a FIDE Album every three years, starting with 1956–58; the first volume contained 603 diagrams.[^s1] Album points became the basis for the composing master titles, though the threshold was only fixed at Leipzig in 1960.[^s1] Membership grew from ten countries in 1956 to nearly forty by the 50th-anniversary meeting in Rhodes in 2007.[^s1]

The decisive change came at the 53rd meeting in Crete, 16–23 October 2010, attended by 31 member countries: the commission adopted the name World Federation for Chess Composition and new statutes allowing registration in Switzerland, and elected Harry Fougiaxis of Greece as president for 2010–2014.[^s3] The statutes describe the WFCC as a non-profit association under Swiss law that regards FIDE as its natural partner, and its titles are still awarded in conjunction with FIDE.[^s2]

## How it works

Each member country is represented by one organisation and one delegate with one vote; the annual WFCC Meeting admits members, grants titles and amends the statutes, while a Presidium of a president and three vice-presidents, elected for four years, runs affairs between meetings.[^s2] The title system has three tracks: Grandmaster, International Master and FIDE Master for chess compositions (earned through published works, chiefly Album selections); International Judge for chess compositions; and International Solving Grandmaster, International Solving Master and FIDE Solving Master (earned through solving ratings and norms).[^s2]

Its competitions fall into two families. Composing events include the World Chess Composition Tournament (WCCT) between countries and the World Championship in Composing for Individuals (WCCI), in which each composer submits a small set of their own works per section to a panel of judges.[^s6] Judges check entries for anticipations and cooks, now with the help of endgame tablebases, and works rated highly enough go into the FIDE Album.[^s6] Solving events centre on the World Chess Solving Championship (WCSC), held with the annual congress.[^s1] The Codex of Chess Composition gives composers conventions they may depart from, while fairy pieces and conditions are largely defined by consensus among composers.[^s6]

## Current state (as of November 2026)

In January 2024 the WFCC announced that its headquarters would move from Geneva to Fujairah in the United Arab Emirates; at that time 41 countries were represented.[^s5] Marjan Kovačević of Serbia, elected president at the 2022 congress in Fujairah, was re-elected at the 68th World Congress in Magdeburg in August 2026, where Danila Pavlov won the individual solving title and Germany the team gold at the 49th WCSC.[^s4]

## Pitfalls for the reader

Two common confusions: the composition titles are separate from the over-the-board titles, since they are earned for published works or solving results rather than games; and the FIDE Album, despite its name, is compiled under the WFCC's authority.[^s1][^s2]

## Connections

The WFCC sits under the general class of chess organisations and is the governing body for chess composition, including chess problems and endgame studies.[^s2] It maintains the Codex and is FIDE's partner organisation, having grown out of a FIDE commission.[^s1][^s5] Its title tracks parallel, but are distinct from, the playing titles listed under chess titles.[^s2]
