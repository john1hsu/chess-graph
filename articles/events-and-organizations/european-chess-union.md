# European Chess Union

The European Chess Union (ECU) is the continental organisation for chess in Europe. It was founded on 30 August 1985 at the Kongresshalle in Graz, Austria, during that year's FIDE Congress, and as of November 2026 it has 54 member national federations.[^s1][^s2] FIDE describes it as one of the most active of its continental federations.[^s5] The ECU presents itself as an independent association with its registered seat at Hünenberg See in Switzerland, and claims the sole right to organise the European chess championships.[^s4]

## Why it was founded

In the early 1980s FIDE's statutes gave the continents no formal influence, and Western European federations felt poorly represented under FIDE President Florencio Campomanes.[^s3] The Swedish official Rolf Littorin asked FIDE for statutory power for Europe, and the union was set up inside FIDE as a body covering the whole continent, East and West, without new financial obligations for its members.[^s2][^s3] The Cold War limited it at first: the Soviet bloc, Poland excepted, stayed out, and Eastern European federations joined only after the political thaw around 1990.[^s2] The ECU's own accounts differ on the size of the founding meeting: the history page gives 29 countries, while Kurt Jungwirth's recollection names 19.[^s2][^s3]

## Leadership

Littorin led the union at its founding and stepped down at the 1986 FIDE Congress in Dubai.[^s2][^s3] The Austrian Kurt Jungwirth then served as president until 1998 and introduced new European championships; Boris Kutin of Slovenia succeeded him, and a permanent office opened in Berlin under Secretary General Horst Metzing.[^s2][^s3] After 2000 the ECU gained financial and administrative autonomy.[^s2] At the General Assembly in Bucharest on 18 July 2026, Zurab Azmaiparashvili was re-elected president with 35 of the 53 votes cast, ahead of Łukasz Turlej (11) and Bachar Kouatly (7); Theodoros Tsorbatzoglou was elected Secretary General.[^s5]

## Competitions

For players, the ECU matters mainly as the organiser of more than twenty events a year.[^s4] They include the European Individual Championship (open and women), the European Team Championship, the European Club Cup and its women's counterpart, European rapid and blitz championships, youth championships from under-8 to under-18, senior, amateur, university, school and city events.[^s4] The individual championship was established in 2000; the pandemic cancelled the 2020 edition, so the 2023 event was the 23rd open championship.[^s8] The union added a European Women's Team Championship in 1992 (Debrecen), a Women's Club Cup in 1996 and an under-16 youth championship in 1998, and the Team Championship moved from a three-year to a two-year cycle.[^s2] The European Club Cup, a six-board competition between national champion clubs, began officially in 1976 as a knockout and has been a Swiss tournament since 2000; it is now run by the ECU under FIDE's patronage.[^s7] Beyond competition, the ECU runs commissions for arbiters, trainers, women's chess, education and Olympic recognition, and programmes such as chess in primary schools.[^s1][^s4]

## Russia and Belarus

In March 2022 the ECU suspended the Russian and Belarusian federations, while letting individual players compete under neutral terms set by its board.[^s6] In April 2022 the Chess Federation of Russia announced that it had applied to join the Asian Chess Federation; the ECU replied that a federation cannot belong to two continents and asked it to withdraw from Europe by 24 February 2023.[^s6]

## Pitfalls

The ECU is not a separate world body: European championship results and titles sit inside the FIDE system, and the union is one of FIDE's four continental groupings.[^s2][^s5] Membership counts, officers and the event calendar change, so any figure should carry a date.[^s1][^s5]

## Connections

The ECU is one of the **chess organizations** and is **part of FIDE** as its European continental body.[^s2][^s5] It organises the **European Individual Chess Championship**, the **European Team Chess Championship** and the **European Chess Club Cup**.[^s4][^s7][^s8] Its dispute with Russia's federation led to that federation's move to the **Asian Chess Federation**.[^s6]
