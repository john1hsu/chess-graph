# Grand Chess Tour

The Grand Chess Tour (GCT) is a yearly circuit of invitational events for a small group of the world's strongest players, run outside the official FIDE cycle. It was unveiled in Saint Louis in April 2015 as a partnership between the Saint Louis club that hosts the Sinquefield Cup, the organisers of Norway Chess and the promoters of the London Chess Classic, with Garry Kasparov credited as its inspiration.[^s1] ChessBase's history of the tour, based on an interview with its long-time executive director Michael Khodarkovsky, dates the idea to 2014, when Kasparov and Khodarkovsky took it to Rex Sinquefield and his team in Saint Louis.[^s5]

## Origins and format changes

The first season in 2015 had three classical round robins: Norway Chess in Stavanger, the Sinquefield Cup in Saint Louis and the London Chess Classic, each a ten-player event with $300,000 in prizes. Players also earned tour points towards a separate $150,000 pool, with an extra $75,000 for the overall champion, for a circuit total of $1,050,000.[^s1] Eight players were committed to all three events, chosen from the January 2015 FIDE rating list, and each host could add a wildcard.[^s1] Contemporary local reporting described the organisers' wider ambition of a major stop on every continent.[^s9]

In 2016 the tour added rapid and blitz legs, partly to bring in new European organisers, and this mixed-time-control identity has defined it since.[^s5] Over the following decade stops ranged from Côte d'Ivoire to Kolkata, usually with simuls and youth events attached.[^s5] By 2026 the circuit had five events in Croatia, Poland, Romania and the United States and a total prize fund of $2 million, with the Super Foundation and the Saint Louis Chess Club as the main non-profit sponsors.[^s2][^s5]

## How a season works

A fixed group of full-tour players, eight in 2026, competes in every leg, joined by wildcard entrants at individual events.[^s2] Points from the regular season decide who reaches the GCT Finals. In 2026 the top four were Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu on 35.5, Wesley So on 34.5, Fabiano Caruana on 29.5 and Vincent Keymer on 28.5, while Javokhir Sindarov missed out on 28.[^s3]

The Finals are a four-player knockout. In 2026 each match had two classical, two rapid (25 minutes plus 10 seconds) and four blitz (5 plus 2) games, with weighted scoring: a classical win was worth 6 points, a rapid win 4 and a blitz win 2, with draws worth half.[^s7] Ties were to be broken by an 8+3 game and then Armageddon.[^s7] The weighting means a player can lose a classical game and still win the match on faster games, which is what happened in 2026.[^s4]

## Recent seasons

The 2026 regular season opened with Hans Niemann winning in Poland; Keymer won the classical Super Chess Classic Romania on his tour debut, Alireza Firouzja won in Croatia, Praggnanandhaa won the Saint Louis Rapid & Blitz and So won the Sinquefield Cup after an Armageddon playoff against Praggnanandhaa.[^s4][^s3] At the Finals in Saint Louis on 22–27 August 2026, Caruana won the first classical game of the final and Praggnanandhaa trailed 3–9 going into the last day, then won both rapid games and held on in blitz to win 15–13.[^s4] He became overall champion with $404,354 in tour earnings, ahead of Caruana and So.[^s2] The 2027 Finals are scheduled for São Paulo from 26 September to 4 October 2027.[^s2] These results are as of November 2026.

Chess.com's list of overall champions runs: Magnus Carlsen (2015, 2017), Wesley So (2016, 2021), Hikaru Nakamura (2018), Ding Liren (2019), Firouzja (2022, 2024) and Caruana (2023, 2025), with no champion listed for 2020.[^s6] Carlsen's official tour page confirms his two titles and shows that in 2022 and 2023 he returned as a wildcard and won rapid and blitz legs in Croatia and Poland.[^s8]

## Pitfalls for readers

Tour points are not tournament scores: a leg's own crosstable, the season standings and the weighted Finals score use three different scales.[^s3][^s7] Because most legs are rapid and blitz, a strong GCT season is not the same thing as classical form: the 2026 champion lost the first classical game of the final and won the title on the faster games.[^s4]

## Connections

The tour is **organised by** the **Saint Louis Chess Club**, which hosts its final legs, and was **developed by** **Garry Kasparov** together with Khodarkovsky.[^s1][^s5] The **Sinquefield Cup** is **part of** it, and **Norway Chess** and the **London Chess Classic** were founding legs.[^s1][^s3] Its legs mix classical with **rapid** and **blitz chess**, and **Armageddon** settles ties.[^s5][^s7] It has been **won by** **Magnus Carlsen**, **Fabiano Caruana** and, in 2026, **Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu**.[^s6][^s4]
