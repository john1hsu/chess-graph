# Chess titles

A chess title is a formal, usually lifelong, recognition that a governing body confers on a player, official or composer who has met published criteria. The best-known titles are the playing titles of FIDE, but FIDE also certifies arbiters and trainers, and separate bodies award titles for chess composition and correspondence chess.[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4][^s5] As of November 2026, FIDE's over-the-board titles are defined by its Title Regulations in force since 1 January 2024, with the Qualification Commission (QC) acting as the final judging unit.[^s1]

## FIDE playing titles

FIDE awards eight titles for standard over-the-board chess, in two parallel ladders. The open ladder runs Grandmaster (GM), International Master (IM), FIDE Master (FM) and Candidate Master (CM); the women's ladder runs Woman Grandmaster (WGM), Woman International Master (WIM), Woman FIDE Master (WFM) and Woman Candidate Master (WCM).[^s1] The women's titles are not restricted versions of the open ones: a woman can hold either kind, and each women's title has its own, lower thresholds.[^s1] A title is valid for life from the date the FIDE Council confirms it, although it can be revoked for ethical abuse of the title or rating system, or removed by the QC if a fair-play breach is found in the events on which it was based.[^s1]

The titles fall into two groups by how they are earned. The four lower titles are **rating titles**: a player qualifies on reaching a published rating, 2300 for FM, 2200 for CM, 2100 for WFM and 2000 for WCM, and for ratings reached after 1 July 2017 the player must also have at least 30 rated games by then.[^s1] The four higher titles are **norm titles**: a player must collect tournament performances (norms) totalling at least 27 games and also reach a minimum rating at some point.[^s1] Any title can alternatively be gained directly for a result in a listed championship, conditionally if the player's rating is below a set minimum.[^s1]

| Title | Norm performance | Opponents' average at least | Rating needed[^s1] |
|---|---|---|---|
| GM | 2600 | 2380 | 2500 |
| IM | 2450 | 2230 | 2400 |
| WGM | 2400 | 2180 | 2300 |
| WIM | 2250 | 2030 | 2200 |

These figures come from the 2024 regulations; every norm also needs at least nine games, a 35% score and a field with enough titled and foreign opponents.[^s1] Rating titles are confirmed administratively by the QC chair on the FIDE Office's advice, whereas norm titles go to the FIDE Council on the QC's recommendation.[^s1]

## Officials and trainers

FIDE's officiating titles are FIDE Arbiter (FA) and the senior International Arbiter (IA); National Arbiter is a federation title that FIDE registers and licenses.[^s2] An FA must attend a FIDE Arbiters' Seminar and pass its exam with at least 80% of the points, and only an FA can apply for IA, on the strength of norms earned at events.[^s2] Arbiters are also graded into categories A to D, the top two open only to IAs, and inactive arbiters are moved to a separate list.[^s2]

The Trainers' Commission awards, from the top, FIDE Senior Trainer, FIDE Trainer, FIDE Instructor, National Instructor and Developmental Instructor, each pitched at students of a different level; the senior title marks a decade or more of high-level service and is not earned at a seminar.[^s3] Trainer titles are awarded for life, but they may be used only with a valid licence, which must be renewed every four years after an initial two-year period.[^s3]

## Composition and correspondence titles

Composers earn titles from points in the FIDE Albums, the anthologies of the best problems and studies published in three-year periods by the World Federation for Chess Composition: 12 points for FIDE Master, 25 for International Master and 70 for Grandmaster of chess composition.[^s4] A selected problem scores one point and a study 1.67, with joint works shared between the composers.[^s4] Correspondence players have their own ladder under the International Correspondence Chess Federation; its 2013 guide gives norm performances of 2600 for GM, 2525 for Senior International Master and 2450 for IM, with separate ladies' titles.[^s5]

## Common misconceptions

A norm is not a title, and holding the required norms still leaves the rating requirement and the 27-game total to be met.[^s1] Conversely, a title is never lost by a later fall in rating; only revocation or a fair-play finding removes it.[^s1] Titles from different bodies are not interchangeable: a correspondence grandmaster or a composition grandmaster is not an over-the-board GM.[^s4][^s5]

## Connections

The system is administered by **FIDE** and rests on the **Elo rating system**, since rating thresholds and performance ratings decide both rating titles and norms.[^s1] The **grandmaster title** is its highest playing rung; the arbiter titles link it to the **arbiter** article, and the album-point titles and ICCF titles connect it to **chess composition** and **correspondence chess**.[^s2][^s4][^s5]
