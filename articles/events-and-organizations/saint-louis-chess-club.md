# Saint Louis Chess Club

The Saint Louis Chess Club is a non-profit 501(c)(3) chess club and scholastic centre in St. Louis, Missouri, and the organiser of most of the strongest invitational chess played in the United States.[^s5][^s6] It opened in July 2008 as the Chess Club and Scholastic Center of Saint Louis, in a building Rex Sinquefield had bought in the Central West End, and it was founded and funded by Rex and Dr. Jeanne Cairns Sinquefield.[^s2][^s4]

## Origins

The club had a modest prehistory. It grew out of the "We're Just Pawns Chess Club", founded in 1995 by Lee Didrikson, and Tony Rich, its executive director at the 2008 opening, had run that club since 1999.[^s1] The new premises at 4657 Maryland Avenue opened officially on 17 July 2008: a storefront with two further floors, about 7,000 square feet of usable space and seating for 150, with plans for weekend tournaments, lectures, blitz and the introduction of chess into school curricula.[^s1] From the start it was organised as a not-for-profit corporation with the Sinquefields as its major funders.[^s1]

## Events

The club quickly became the home of American championship chess. Saint Louis hosted the U.S. Championship and U.S. Women's Championship from 2009, and the U.S. Junior Closed from 2010, as recorded in a 2014 Senate resolution.[^s3] By 2018 the city had held the U.S. Championship ten years running, and the club still describes itself as home of the U.S. Championship; its 2026 calendar also lists the senior, junior and girls' national championships.[^s2][^s6]

At the elite level, the club created the Sinquefield Cup, whose first edition in September 2013 had four players, and the Cairns Cup, an elite women's event.[^s2][^s4] The Sinquefield Cup led on to the Grand Chess Tour: Garry Kasparov and Michael Khodarkovsky pitched the idea to Sinquefield and his team in Saint Louis in 2014, and the tour launched in 2015 with classical events in Norway, Saint Louis and London, adding rapid and blitz in 2016.[^s2][^s7] As of 2026 the tour is described as a non-profit venture driven by the club and the Superbet Foundation, and the club's 2026 schedule included the Saint Louis Rapid & Blitz, the Sinquefield Cup and the Grand Chess Tour Finals.[^s6][^s7]

## Education and outreach

Scholastic chess is central to the club's mission. The 2014 resolution described free classes, weekly tournaments, camps and support for after-school programmes across more than 100 Saint Louis schools and community centres.[^s3] A 2018 Hall of Fame exhibition counted programmes in about 140 schools, including free weekly lessons for 2,410 pupils in 35 St. Louis Public Schools that spring.[^s2] In 2025 the club said it had introduced chess to more than 100,000 students in its area.[^s5]

## The Chess Capital

On 5 May 2014 the U.S. Senate agreed to S. Res. 102, supporting the designation of Saint Louis as the "National Chess Capital" and urging schools nationwide to adopt chess programmes.[^s3] A 2018 World Chess Hall of Fame exhibition linked the club's growth to wider American results: the 2016 Olympiad gold, three Americans in the world top ten, and strong players such as Fabiano Caruana and Wesley So settling in the city.[^s2] In autumn 2025 the club reopened an expanded campus of about 30,000 square feet, with a tournament hall, classrooms and a broadcast studio; the announced opening programme was a Kasparov–Anand Chess960 exhibition followed by the U.S. Championships.[^s5]

## Pitfalls

Two cautions for readers. First, "Saint Louis" in tournament names usually means the club's events, but the club organises some Grand Chess Tour legs abroad, so check the venue.[^s6] Second, figures on schools, students and members come from the club's own announcements and change year by year; cite them with a date.[^s2][^s5]

## Connections

The club belongs among the **chess organizations**. It hosts the **U.S. Chess Championship**, and **US Chess** named Saint Louis its Chess City of the Year in 2009 and 2011 and the club its Chess Club of the Year in 2010.[^s3][^s6] It shares the Saint Louis Chess Campus in the Central West End with the **World Chess Hall of Fame**.[^s8] It created the **Sinquefield Cup** and, with **Garry Kasparov** as co-founder, drives the **Grand Chess Tour**.[^s2][^s7]
