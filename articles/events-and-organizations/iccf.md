# ICCF

The International Correspondence Chess Federation (ICCF) is to correspondence chess what FIDE is to over-the-board play: the body that writes the international rules, organises the individual and team world championships, keeps a rating list and confers titles on players and arbiters.[^s1] Its statutes define it as an independent, non-profit organisation with its legal seat in Switzerland, and they give it the motto *Amici Sumus* ("we are friends").[^s1] It is an affiliated international member of FIDE, and FIDE recognises its titles, championships and ratings.[^s1][^s4]

## Origins

International correspondence play was organised long before the present federation. The Internationaler Fernschachbund (IFSB), founded in 1928, was the first international correspondence league; after the Second World War it was succeeded by the International Correspondence Chess Association (ICCA) in 1945, which in turn was refounded as the ICCF in 1951.[^s4][^s5] The team competition is older than the ICCF name itself: the first correspondence olympiad began in 1949 and was won by Hungary, and by the AJEC account 22 olympiads had been held, with later titles going to teams including the USSR, Czechoslovakia, Great Britain, Germany and Norway.[^s5]

Eight presidents led the federation up to the 2010s, from Jean-Louis Ormond of Switzerland (1951–1953) through Hans-Werner von Massow of Germany, whose term ran from 1960 to 1987, to Éric Ruch of France, president from 2009.[^s4] The same history notes a structure of more than 50 national member federations grouped into four zones (Europe, Latin America, North America/Pacific and Africa/Asia).[^s4]

## How it is governed

Membership is by country: each nation is represented by one national correspondence chess organisation, admitted by Congress, which appoints a delegate, votes and enters teams and players.[^s1] Congress is the supreme, legislative body and must meet at least every second year, in person or online.[^s1] Between congresses an elected Executive Board runs the federation; under the 2026 electoral rules its posts are President, General Secretary, Finance Director, World Tournament Director, Marketing Director, Services Director and Rules Director, with an Auditor elected alongside them by secret ballot.[^s3] Board terms are four years.[^s1] Each voting member has one vote, absent federations may vote online or by proxy, and no person may cast more than three votes.[^s3] Disputes go to two separate bodies, an Appeals Committee for playing-rule questions and an Arbitration Committee for conduct, whose decisions are final.[^s1]

## The playing rules

The ICCF Rules differ sharply from the over-the-board Laws. The standard server time control is 50 days per 10 moves (40 days for postal play), and players may take up to 45 days of leave a year in most events.[^s2] Players may consult any publicly available source of information, which includes engines, databases and books, but may not let another person analyse or choose their moves.[^s2] All ICCF events allow win and draw claims based on 7-piece tablebases, and a tablebase win overrides the 50-move rule.[^s2] Ratings carry a rating deviation, a rating counts as "fixed" once that deviation is 120 or lower, and title eligibility requires a fixed rating.[^s2] Titles range from correspondence master norms up to Grandmaster, with separate Ladies titles and an International Arbiter title for tournament directors.[^s2]

## Recent changes (as of November 2026)

The 2025 Congress banned holding accounts under more than one name, required rated tournaments to allow computer use, tightened the handling of games left by a player who dies mid-event, and declined a rise in entry fees.[^s6] Rule details change at most congresses, so the rules in force should always be checked in the current edition.[^s2]

## Pitfalls

Two misunderstandings are common. First, engine use is not cheating in ICCF play: it is explicitly allowed, and the skill lies in steering and judging the analysis.[^s2] Second, ICCF titles are correspondence titles; an ICCF Grandmaster has not thereby earned the FIDE over-the-board title.[^s1][^s4]

## Connections

The ICCF is one of the specialist bodies under **chess organisations** and is the governing body of **correspondence chess**, organising the **World Correspondence Chess Championship** and the correspondence **olympiads**.[^s1][^s5] It is a member of **FIDE**, and its rules contrast with the **FIDE Laws of Chess** in permitting engine help and **endgame tablebase** claims, which ties it to **computer chess**.[^s1][^s2] Its correspondence titles sit beside the over-the-board ones described under **chess titles**.[^s2]
