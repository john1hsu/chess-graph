# Chess Olympiad

The Chess Olympiad is the team championship of the chess world: national federations send teams to a single venue, where an Open section and a Women's section are played in parallel.[^s1] FIDE organises it, and the event has been held since 1927 and every two years since 1950.[^s3] For a club player it is the most visible place where grandmasters and amateurs from small federations play in the same hall, often in the same round.

## Origins

FIDE was founded in Paris in July 1924, and an unofficial international team tournament held there in June 1924 is counted as an unofficial Olympiad; Czechoslovakia won it.[^s2] A second attempt in Budapest in 1926 drew only four countries because invitations went out late.[^s3] The first official Olympiad, then called the Tournament of Nations, was played at Westminster Central Hall in London from 18 to 30 July 1927, with sixteen teams in a fifteen-round all-play-all.[^s4] Hungary, led by Géza Maróczy, won ahead of Denmark and Great Britain and received the Hamilton-Russell Cup, a trophy donated by F. G. Hamilton-Russell that has rotated between Open winners ever since.[^s2][^s4]

The Soviet Union first took part at Helsinki in 1952, where a team including Keres, Smyslov, Bronstein and Geller won gold; Soviet teams then dominated for about four decades.[^s5] A separate Women's Olympiad began at Emmen in the Netherlands in 1957, with 21 teams, and was also won by the USSR.[^s2][^s3] Politics intruded too: the USSR, Hungary and other East European federations stayed away from Haifa 1976, which the United States won, and a rival event was held in Tripoli.[^s2][^s6] During the pandemic FIDE ran a first Online Olympiad in 2020, in which Russia and India shared first place.[^s2]

## How it works

Under the FIDE regulations each team has four players and one reserve, and both sections are eleven-round Swiss tournaments paired under special Olympiad pairing rules.[^s1] A match is decided over the four boards: the team scoring more game points takes 2 match points, a 2–2 draw gives each team 1, and a loss gives 0. Standings are by match points, not game points.[^s1] Ties are broken first by the sum of opponents' adjusted scores weighted by the game points scored against them, then by total game points, then by opponents' match points.[^s1] The 2022 time control was 90 minutes for 40 moves plus 30 minutes, with a 30-second increment from move one.[^s1] The Open winner receives the Hamilton-Russell Cup, the Women's winner the Vera Menchik Cup, and the best combined result the Nona Gaprindashvili Trophy; individual medals go to the best performers on each board.[^s1]

The Swiss format copes with very large and uneven fields: Haifa 1976 was already a thirteen-round Swiss with 48 teams, and the Chennai organisers noted 185 teams at Batumi 2018.[^s6][^s3]

## Recent editions

At Budapest in 2024 (the 45th Olympiad) India won both sections, the Open team with 21 match points and the Women's team with 19; Gukesh Dommaraju took the individual gold on board one with 9 points from 10 games.[^s7] The 46th Olympiad was held in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, from 15 to 27 September 2026.[^s9] As of November 2026 the latest champions are Uzbekistan in the Open section, its second title after Chennai 2022, secured by a 2.5–1.5 final-round win over Ukraine, ahead of India and Germany; and China in the Women's section with 20 match points, ahead of Kazakhstan and India.[^s8][^s9] The next Olympiad is scheduled for the United Arab Emirates in 2028.[^s9]

## Pitfalls for readers and players

Match points make the Olympiad unlike an individual Swiss. A team that wins three matches 2.5–1.5 outscores one that wins two 4–0 and loses one narrowly, so what counts is winning the match, not the margin.[^s1] Medal tables also need care: ties on match points are common, and in 2026 India took Open silver over Germany and Women's bronze over four other teams only on tie-break.[^s9] Finally, "first Olympiad" can mean 1924, 1926 or 1927; FIDE's own museum counts 1924 as unofficial and 1927 as the first official edition.[^s2]

## Connections

The Olympiad is the flagship **team chess** competition and is **sanctioned by FIDE**, which organises it.[^s1][^s2] Its games are played under the **FIDE Laws of Chess**, and its pairings and rankings rely on the **Swiss system** and FIDE's **tie-break regulations**.[^s1] It sits beside the **World Chess Championship** as FIDE's other historic flagship event, and recent editions have been a stage for players such as **Gukesh Dommaraju**, who won board-one gold in 2024.[^s7]
