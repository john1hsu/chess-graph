# U.S. Chess Championship

The U.S. Chess Championship is the national title event for American players. Today it is an invitational, classical round robin: the 2025 and 2026 editions each had twelve players in a single all-play-all over eleven rounds, run alongside a U.S. Women's Championship of the same size.[^s7][^s8] US Chess awards the championships to the organiser that offers the best planning and execution, and the Saint Louis Chess Club has staged every edition since 2009.[^s4][^s5]

## From title matches to a tournament

The organisers date the championship back to 1845.[^s5] For most of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, though, there was no settled way of deciding who the national champion was, and claims to the title were often disputed. In 1888, for example, Jackson Showalter won a tournament of state champions run by the United States Chess Association and became its champion, but that was not accepted as the overall national title.[^s1] Frank Marshall accepted the title in 1909 and kept it until 1936, the longest and least contested reign in its history; he then gave it up in favour of a championship tournament, and Samuel Reshevsky became the first champion under the new format.[^s2] The National Chess Federation, formed in 1926, had already pushed the title towards an official status decided by regular tournaments, a role later inherited by US Chess.[^s1]

## Records

Reshevsky won the first five tournaments, from 1936 to 1942, and no one has matched that streak since. He and Bobby Fischer share the record of eight titles. Walter Browne has six, and Fabiano Caruana, Jackson Showalter, Larry Evans, Gata Kamsky and Hikaru Nakamura have five each (as of October 2026).[^s6] Fischer won every championship he entered. In 1957/58 he became the youngest player to take first place, and in 1963/64 he scored the only perfect result in the event's history.[^s3]

## The Saint Louis era

Since 2009 the club has hosted the U.S. Championship, the U.S. Women's Championship and the U.S. Junior Closed together, a trio it calls the "Triple Crown" of American chess. Nakamura and Kamsky shared the titles from 2009 to 2014.[^s4] The prize funds are among the largest for any national championship: the 2025 Open and Women's events paid out $402,000 combined.[^s7] In 2026 the Open fund was $250,000, with $55,000 for first place.[^s8]

The field is built from several qualifying routes. The defending champion returns, the U.S. Junior Closed winner is invited, and in 2026 the rest of the field came from top-rated players on the US Chess invitational rating list, a wildcard and the winner of the U.S. Open.[^s4][^s5] Ties for first are settled by a playoff of two rapid games, followed by blitz if needed.[^s8]

## Recent champions

Caruana won his first title in 2016 and then four in a row from 2022 to 2025. His 2024 score was officially 7/10 after an expelled player's results were annulled. He won in 2025 with 8/11, half a point ahead of Wesley So.[^s6][^s7] In 2026 (9–22 October) So and Caruana tied on 7/11. In the rapid playoff So drew the first game and won the second, taking the match 1.5–0.5.[^s8][^s9] That gave So his fourth U.S. title, after 2017, 2020 and 2021.[^s9] In the Women's event, WIM Rachael Li won on her debut and became its youngest champion.[^s8] As of November 2026, So and Li are the reigning champions.[^s8]

## Why it matters for club players

For an American player, the championship is the top of a domestic ladder that starts at rated club events. Places come through rating, junior and open-tournament results, so the U.S. Open and the Junior Closed are realistic stepping stones for strong young players.[^s4][^s5] Its round-robin format, in which every player meets every other, also makes the title harder to win on pairing luck than a large Swiss would.[^s7][^s8]

## Connections

The championship is a kind of **chess tournament**, held under the authority of **US Chess** and hosted by the **Saint Louis Chess Club**.[^s4][^s5] Its record holders include **Frank Marshall**, the longest-reigning match-era champion; **Samuel Reshevsky**, the first tournament champion; and **Bobby Fischer**, who shares the record for most titles.[^s2][^s3][^s6] It is played alongside the **U.S. Women's Chess Championship**, and its playoffs use a rapid-and-blitz **tie-break**.[^s4][^s8]
