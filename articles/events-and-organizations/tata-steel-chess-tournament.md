# Tata Steel Chess Tournament

The Tata Steel Chess Tournament is a January chess festival in the Dutch seaside village of Wijk aan Zee, and its top group is among the most coveted invitational events in the game; its organisers and FIDE both call it the "Wimbledon of Chess".[^s2][^s4] What sets it apart is the mix: the world's elite play in the same hall as hundreds of amateurs, so a club player can sit a few metres from a world champion.[^s1] For a serious amateur it is also the classic example of a long, closed round robin, where consistency over thirteen rounds matters more than one brilliant day.

## From works tournament to elite event

The event began in 1938 as a tournament for employees of the steel company Koninklijke Hoogovens, played at its site in Beverwijk; Philip Bakker and Jilling van Dijk shared first place.[^s1] That first "Hoogovens Schaaktoernooi" had just four players, and the shared winners scored 2½/3.[^s6] It continued through the Second World War, and the list of champions shows how quickly it broadened: Max Euwe won in 1940 and 1942, the Belgian Alberic O'Kelly de Galway took the 1946 edition, and Savielly Tartakower won in 1949.[^s3][^s6]

In 1968 the tournament moved to Wijk aan Zee, at first in the Hotel Kennemerduin; since 1979 it has been held in the De Moriaan community centre, where the 2026 Masters was again played.[^s1][^s4] The name has followed the steelworks' owners: the Hoogovens tournament until 1999, the Corus tournament from 2000, and then the Tata Steel Chess Tournament after the company passed to Tata Steel.[^s1] Older game databases therefore record the same event as "Hoogovens", "Corus" or "Tata Steel", which is worth knowing when searching for a game.

## Format

The Masters is a 14-player single round robin of 13 rounds.[^s2][^s5] In 2026 the time control was 120 minutes for 40 moves, then 30 minutes for the rest of the game with a 30-second increment from move 41, and draw offers were not allowed before move 40.[^s5] A second 14-player round robin, the Challengers, gives rising players and veterans a route upward: its winner is invited to the next year's Masters.[^s4][^s5] Around them run amateur groups, a two-day family chess festival on the second weekend, and since 2015 a simultaneous display in which local children face professionals.[^s1]

## Record winners

Magnus Carlsen holds the record with eight titles: 2008 (shared with Levon Aronian), 2010, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2018, 2019 and 2022.[^s3][^s2] Viswanathan Anand won five times, starting with a shared first in 1989, his debut year in the top group.[^s3][^s6] Garry Kasparov played the event three times and won all three, in 1999, 2000 and 2001.[^s3][^s6] Lajos Portisch won four times, and two Dutch stalwarts, Jan Hein Donner and Loek van Wely, have each played 298 games there, the most of anyone.[^s6]

Home winners have been rare in the modern era: after Jan Timman's win in 1985, the next Dutch champions were Jorden van Foreest in 2021 and Anish Giri in 2023.[^s1][^s3] Wei Yi won in 2024 and Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu in 2025.[^s3]

## The 2026 edition

The 88th edition ran from mid-January to 1 February 2026.[^s4][^s5] Nodirbek Abdusattorov won the Masters on his fourth attempt with 9/13, beating Arjun Erigaisi in the final round to finish half a point ahead of Javokhir Sindarov; Jorden van Foreest was third on tie-break.[^s5] Abdusattorov had come close in each of the three previous editions, including a play-off in 2024.[^s4][^s5] The Challengers went to 15-year-old Andy Woodward with 10/13, half a point ahead of Vasyl Ivanchuk, earning him a place in the 2027 Masters.[^s5] The 89th edition is scheduled for 15–31 January 2027.[^s4] These facts are as of November 2026.

## Pitfalls for readers

Wijk aan Zee rewards stamina over a three-week event, and the scoring margins are thin: in 2026 three players tied for third, separated only by tie-break, a full point behind the runner-up.[^s5] Because there is no play-off in the Challengers, a player can also finish half a point short with the best tie-break of the chasing group and get nothing for it, as Ivanchuk found in 2026.[^s5] Finally, statistics quoted in older articles go stale: the 2018 ChessBase survey had Carlsen and Anand level on five wins each, but Carlsen has since taken his total to eight.[^s6][^s3]

## Connections

The event is an **instance of** the closed **chess tournament** and its Masters is a classic **round robin**, played under the **FIDE Laws of Chess**.[^s2][^s5] It is **won by** record-holder **Magnus Carlsen**, by **Viswanathan Anand** and **Garry Kasparov**, and in 2026 by **Nodirbek Abdusattorov**, with **Javokhir Sindarov** as runner-up that year.[^s3][^s5] Its Challengers group acts as a ladder into that elite, since the winner is invited to the following Masters.[^s4][^s5]
