# Grandmaster title

Grandmaster (GM) is the top playing title that FIDE awards; in FIDE's hierarchy only the World Champion stands above it.[^s4] Like all FIDE titles it is held for life from the date the FIDE Council confirms it, although it can be revoked for serious ethical abuse of the title or rating system, or removed if a fair-play breach is found in the events behind the application.[^s1] As of November 2026 the rules are set by the FIDE Title Regulations in force since 1 January 2024, with the Qualification Commission (QC) as the final judging body.[^s1]

## Two routes to the title

Most grandmasters qualify through **norms**: tournament performances that meet a set of strict conditions. Titles based on norms are awarded by the FIDE Council on the QC's recommendation.[^s1] The applicant needs norms covering at least 27 games in total and must have reached a rating of 2500 or more at some point; that rating need not appear on a published list, but the federation then has to prove it.[^s1] For applications that include a norm made after 30 June 2022, at least one norm must come from a listed category of event, such as the Chess Olympiad, a qualifier for the Candidates or the World Cup, or a final national championship.[^s1]

The other route is a **direct title** for a result in a listed event. Under the table in force from 1 January 2026, reaching the last 16 of the World Cup gives the GM title, as does winning the Women's World Cup, the World Under-20 or Under-18 championship, a World Senior championship (over 50 or over 65), a continental individual championship or the Arab individual championship.[^s2] A joint first place in several of these events earns a GM norm instead, and some junior and senior events give only a norm.[^s2] A direct title is immediate only if the player has reached a rating of at least 2300; otherwise it is conditional until that rating is achieved.[^s1]

## What a grandmaster norm requires

A norm event must have at least nine games, and events of more than 13 rounds count as only 13 games.[^s1] The field must be international: at least two federations other than the player's own, no more than three-fifths of opponents from the player's federation and no more than two-thirds from any single federation.[^s1] At least half the opponents must hold titles (Candidate Master and Woman Candidate Master excluded), and at least a third of them, with a minimum of three, must be grandmasters.[^s1] The opponents must average at least 2380; one low-rated opponent may be raised to a floor of 2200 for the calculation, and an unrated opponent counts as 1400.[^s1] Finally the player needs a performance rating of 2600 (2599.5 before rounding) and a score of at least 35%.[^s1]

A worked example shows how tight this is. In a nine-round open, a player chasing a norm must have met at least five titled opponents, at least three of them grandmasters, and opponents from at least two other federations; even a 2600 performance fails if the average falls below 2380 because the pairings produced too many weaker opponents.[^s1] This is why norm-seekers often prefer closed round-robins designed to meet the conditions, and why a single unlucky pairing in a Swiss can end a norm chance.

## History

The word predates the title by more than a century: the earliest known chess use, found by Edward Winter, is from *Bell's Life* in 1838, and later writers applied it to Philidor.[^s5] The popular story that Tsar Nicholas II conferred the title on the finalists at St Petersburg 1914 has never been documented; Winter traces it to a 1940 magazine article and rates it "not proven".[^s5] FIDE created the formal title at its 1949 Paris Congress and first awarded it in 1950 to 27 players.[^s3][^s5] That first list mixed active leaders such as World Champion Mikhail Botvinnik with veterans honoured for past achievements, among them Rubinstein, Maróczy and Tartakower; written norm criteria followed only in 1957.[^s6] The number of grandmasters has grown steadily since, and Britannica gives the total only as more than 500.[^s4]

## Pitfalls

A norm is not a title: a player can hold three norms and still need the 2500 rating, and the 27-game and event-category conditions are checked only when the federation applies.[^s1] The 2500 threshold is a one-time requirement, not a rating a grandmaster must keep.[^s1] And Woman Grandmaster (WGM) is a separate title with a lower bar, a 2400 norm performance, rather than a women's version of GM; winning the Women's World Cup, for example, gives the open GM title directly.[^s1][^s2]

## Connections

The grandmaster title is one of the **chess titles** and is awarded by **FIDE**, which sets it out in the **FIDE Title Regulations**.[^s1] It rests on the **Elo rating system** through the 2500 requirement and the performance-rating calculation, and sits above the **International Master title** in the norm ladder.[^s1][^s4] The QC's power to strip a title after a fair-play finding links it to the **fair-play regulations**.[^s1]
