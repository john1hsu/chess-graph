# World Blitz Championship

The World Blitz Championship is FIDE's world title for blitz chess. Like the rapid title, it is won in a single annual event rather than defended in a challenge match, and since 2012 it has been staged together with the World Rapid Championship in the last days of December.[^s1] The 2025 edition in Doha ran from 25 to 30 December, with the blitz semifinals and finals on the 30th.[^s3][^s2]

## History

FIDE counts the 1988 event in Saint John, Canada, as the first championship. It was a 32-player knockout of four-game matches, and Mikhail Tal won it, beating Levon Vaganian 3½–½ in the final; Anatoly Karpov and Garry Kasparov both went out before the last stage.[^s1] A follow-up event planned for 1989 was cancelled, and the title lapsed until 2006, when Alexander Grischuk won in Rishon LeZion after a sudden-death final against Peter Svidler.[^s1] Champions over the following years included Vassily Ivanchuk (2007), Leinier Domínguez (2008), Magnus Carlsen (2009, with 31/42 in a double round robin) and Levon Aronian (2010).[^s1]

The 2011 event did not take place, and from 2012 blitz and rapid were merged into one championship week. Grischuk won again in Astana in 2012, and the 2013 edition in Khanty-Mansiysk, won by Le Quang Liem, was the first played as a Swiss open with 60 players.[^s1] Later Swiss winners listed by FIDE include Carlsen (2014, 2017, 2018), Grischuk (2015) and Sergey Karjakin, who edged Carlsen on tie-break in Doha in 2016.[^s1] In Warsaw in 2021 Maxime Vachier-Lagrave tied on 15/21 with Jan-Krzysztof Duda and Alireza Firouzja and then beat Duda in a play-off, while the defending champion Carlsen finished twelfth.[^s5] Carlsen returned to win in Samarkand in 2023 with 16/21, half a point ahead of Daniil Dubov, for his seventh blitz title.[^s6]

## The shared title of 2024

For 2024 in New York FIDE added a knockout of the top eight from each section.[^s2] The open final between Carlsen and Ian Nepomniachtchi stood 2–2 after four games, and three sudden-death games then failed to separate them. Carlsen proposed sharing the title, Nepomniachtchi agreed, and FIDE President Arkady Dvorkovich approved it under the clause letting him decide cases the regulations do not foresee; both were credited with first place and no silver medal was given.[^s4] Ju Wenjun won the women's title, beating Lei Tingjie in a tie-break game after five draws.[^s4] The episode is unusual for a world championship, and the decision showed how far the event's final stage depended on discretion rather than on a fixed rule.[^s4]

## Current format

For 2025 FIDE reworked the blitz while leaving the rapid unchanged. The open Swiss was lengthened to 19 rounds and the women's to 15, each over two days, and only the top four now reach the knockout, which starts at the semifinals.[^s2] Semifinals and final are four-game matches.[^s3] FIDE's chief executive Emil Sutovsky argued that more Swiss rounds would make sure the strongest players reach the knockout, and the final day was timed for audiences in the main chess regions.[^s2] Of the €1 million total prize fund, €350,000 went to the open blitz and €150,000 to the women's blitz.[^s2]

## Recent results

As of November 2026 the reigning champions are Carlsen and Bibisara Assaubayeva, both crowned in Doha in December 2025.[^s3] Arjun Erigaisi topped the open Swiss with 15/19, ahead of Fabiano Caruana and Carlsen, and Nodirbek Abdusattorov took the last place on tie-break.[^s7] Carlsen beat Caruana in the semifinal and then Abdusattorov 2½–1½ in the final after losing the first game; FIDE described it as his ninth world blitz title.[^s3] Assaubayeva, who first won as a 17-year-old in Warsaw in 2021, beat Zhu Jiner 3–0 and Anna Muzychuk 2½–1½ for her third women's blitz crown.[^s3][^s5]

## What it rewards

Nineteen blitz games in two days reward fast pattern recognition and steady nerves more than deep calculation, and the Swiss stage gives little room to recover from a bad patch: in Doha six players tied for fourth place on 13/19 and tie-break alone decided which of them went through.[^s3] The knockout then turns the title into short matches in which a single game swings the result, as Abdusattorov's first-game win against Carlsen showed.[^s3][^s7]

## Connections

The championship is the flagship event of blitz chess and is sanctioned by FIDE, which sets its format each year.[^s2] It runs as a chess tournament on the Swiss system, with its own tie-break order deciding the qualifiers, and it shares venue and dates with the World Rapid Championship.[^s2][^s7] Its champions include Tal, the first winner, and Carlsen, the most frequent one; Nepomniachtchi shared the 2024 title, and Ju Wenjun won the women's event that year.[^s1][^s3][^s4] It is separate from the classical World Chess Championship, which is still decided by a title match.[^s1]
