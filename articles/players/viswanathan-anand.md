# Viswanathan Anand

Viswanathan "Vishy" Anand is an Indian grandmaster, born in Madras (now Chennai) on 11 December 1969, who learned the game from his mother at six.[^s1] He was India's first grandmaster and is counted by FIDE among the world champions, having held a version of the world title from 2000 and the undisputed title from 2007 until 2013.[^s1][^s2] In India he was nicknamed the "Lightning Kid" for the speed of his calculation and his many fast-chess successes.[^s1]

## Early career

Anand won the Indian sub-junior championship with a perfect 9/9 at 14, became India's youngest international master at 15, and then won three national championships in a row.[^s1] In 1987, aged 17, he won the World Junior Championship, which Britannica describes as the first world chess title won by an Asian player, and he received the grandmaster title in 1988.[^s1] His first major international tournament victory came in 1991, ahead of both Garry Kasparov and Anatoly Karpov.[^s1]

## Title challenges in the split era

During the years when the title was divided between FIDE and Kasparov's PCA, Anand reached several finals before winning one. He lost the 1995 PCA match to Kasparov (one win, four losses, thirteen draws) and the 1998 FIDE final to Karpov, which was decided in rapid tiebreak games after the six classical games had ended level.[^s1] In 2000 he took the FIDE knockout championship by winning seven consecutive matches, the final against Alexei Shirov by 3½–½.[^s2]

## Undisputed champion (2007–2013)

In April 2007 Anand topped the FIDE rating list for the first time with 2786, the sixth player to lead the list since 1970 and the first from Asia.[^s5] Later that year he won the 2007 World Championship, a double round-robin tournament that included the reigning champion Vladimir Kramnik, finishing as the only unbeaten player.[^s2] He then defended the title three times: against Kramnik in Bonn in 2008 (6½–4½), against Veselin Topalov in Sofia in 2010 (6½–5½, decided in the twelfth and final game), and against Boris Gelfand in Moscow in 2012, where the classical games were tied and Anand won the rapid tiebreak.[^s1][^s2]

Magnus Carlsen took the title from him in November 2013, winning after ten of the twelve scheduled games.[^s1][^s2] Anand responded by winning the next Candidates Tournament in Khanty-Mansiysk with a round to spare, but lost the 2014 rematch to Carlsen.[^s2]

## Later career and FIDE role

Anand continued to compete at the top level after losing the title. At the King Salman World Rapid Championship in Riyadh in December 2017 he shared first place with Vladimir Fedoseev and won the play-off 1½–½ in two blitz games to become world rapid champion.[^s4] In August 2022, at the FIDE General Assembly in Chennai, he was elected FIDE Deputy President on Arkady Dvorkovich's ticket.[^s3] He still plays selected events: in January 2026 he finished second, behind Nihal Sarin, in the rapid section of Tata Steel Chess India in Kolkata.[^s7] FIDE rating data shown by Lichess in November 2026 lists him at 2739 classical, 2721 rapid and 2706 blitz (as of November 2026).[^s8]

His contemporaries rate him very highly: the FIDE museum records Kramnik calling him perhaps the most naturally talented player in about fifty years, and Levon Aronian remarking that Anand "always tricks you".[^s2]

## Example: Kramnik–Anand, Bonn 2008, game 3

The game that put Anand ahead in the 2008 match shows his preparation and dynamic style. In the Meran Variation (ECO D49) Black accepted a ruined kingside and an exposed king in exchange for a fast attack with rook, bishops and queen against White's king; Anand won in 41 moves to lead 2–1.[^s6]

## Connections

Anand sits under the World Chess Championship as a holder of the world champion title, which he took in the 2007 tournament that included Kramnik and lost to Magnus Carlsen in 2013; his two title matches against Carlsen and his 1995 match with Kasparov tie him to the other modern champions.[^s1][^s2] He holds the grandmaster title, played in and won the Candidates Tournament, and serves FIDE as Deputy President.[^s1][^s2][^s3] His 2017 world rapid title links him to rapid chess, and the Bonn 2008 game above, a Meran line from the Slav Defence family, is a well-known example of his match preparation.[^s4][^s6] Within Indian chess he was the pioneer, the country's first grandmaster and first world junior champion.[^s1][^s2]
