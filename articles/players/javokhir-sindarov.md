# Javokhir Sindarov

Javokhir Sindarov is an Uzbek grandmaster, born in Tashkent on 8 December 2005, who rose from child prodigy to world-championship challenger in the space of about seven years.[^s1][^s3] Between November 2025 and April 2026 he won the FIDE World Cup and the Candidates Tournament in succession, and he is the first Uzbek player to qualify for a match for the undisputed world title.[^s4][^s8]

## Prodigy and grandmaster title

Sindarov collected his three grandmaster norms in 2018: in Voronezh in June, at the World Junior Championship in Turkey in September, and at a First Saturday tournament in Budapest in October.[^s1] Having also reached a 2500 rating on the October 2018 list, he qualified for the title aged 12 years, 10 months and 8 days, at the time the second-youngest grandmaster in history; later prodigies have since pushed him down to fifth on that list.[^s1][^s3] He crossed 2600 in December 2021 and briefly touched 2701 in December 2023.[^s3]

## Olympiads and earlier results

At 16 he was the youngest member of the young Uzbekistan team, coached by Ivan Sokolov, that won the 44th Chess Olympiad in Chennai in 2022; one of his wins there, against Armenia's Samvel Ter-Sahakyan, featured a knight sacrifice on f5 that he credited to deep preparation.[^s2] Four years later, on home soil in Samarkand, he played board two in Uzbekistan's second Olympiad gold, took the individual bronze on that board and drew with Vasyl Ivanchuk in the deciding final-round match against Ukraine.[^s9] In knockout and Swiss events he built a reputation as a dangerous outsider: he knocked Alireza Firouzja out of the 2021 World Cup, beat Maxime Vachier-Lagrave at the 2023 World Cup and finished eighth at the 2023 Grand Swiss after starting as the 43rd seed.[^s3]

## World Cup winner (2025)

His 2025 season brought a Bundesliga title with Düsseldorfer SK (an unbeaten 12/14), outright first at TePe Sigeman & Co. and a share of first at the UzChess Cup; his rating rose from 2692 to a career-high 2726 over the year, from 54 classical games with only two losses.[^s3] The decisive event was the 206-player FIDE World Cup in Goa, which he went through unbeaten in classical games.[^s3][^s4] In the final against Wei Yi both classical games were drawn; after a drawn first rapid game, Sindarov won the second with Black when Wei, short of time, blundered with 57.Rxd4?? and was met by 57...Qh4+.[^s4] He became the youngest World Cup champion in the event's history and qualified for the 2026 Candidates.[^s3][^s4]

## Candidates winner (2026)

At the Candidates in Cyprus he took the lead early: a fourth-round win over co-leader Fabiano Caruana put him on 3½/4, a full point clear.[^s6] He secured first place with a round to spare and finished unbeaten on 10/14 (six wins, eight draws), the highest score since the event became a double round-robin in 2013.[^s5][^s8] Anish Giri took second place and Caruana third, on 7½.[^s5]

As of November 2026, Sindarov is rated 2782 and ranked fourth in the world, 72 points and 22 places above the reigning champion Gukesh D; Chess.com calls the ranking gap a record for a title match since the title was reunified in 2006.[^s10] The match is scheduled for 22 November–12 December 2026 at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny, Geneva, over up to 14 classical games (first to 7½ points) with rapid and blitz tiebreaks at 7–7; its result was not known when this article was verified.[^s8]

## Example: Sindarov–Caruana, Candidates 2026, round 4

In a Queen's Gambit Accepted (ECO D26), Sindarov chose 10.a4, a move he had prepared for the game, and his understanding of the resulting structures cost Caruana about an hour on the clock by move 18.[^s6][^s7] Black recaptured 12...gxf6 and kept his king in the centre for many moves; the pawn break 27.d5 won material and left White dominant; Caruana resigned at move 36 with under a minute left.[^s6][^s7]

## Connections

Sindarov sits under the World Chess Championship as the 2026 challenger to Gukesh Dommaraju.[^s8] His route there ran through the FIDE World Cup, which he won in 2025, and the Candidates Tournament, which he won in 2026; both are FIDE qualifying events for the title cycle.[^s4][^s5] He holds the grandmaster title, earned at 12, and has played in two Olympiad-winning Uzbekistan teams.[^s1][^s2][^s9] His round-four Candidates win over Caruana is a model example of a prepared Queen's Gambit Accepted line.[^s6][^s7]
