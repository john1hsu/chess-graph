# Ju Wenjun

Ju Wenjun is a Chinese grandmaster, born in Shanghai on 31 January 1991, who has been Women's World Chess Champion since May 2018.[^s1] FIDE awarded her the Woman Grandmaster title in 2009 and the full Grandmaster title in 2014.[^s1] In 2017 she became only the fifth woman to reach a 2600 rating.[^s2] As of November 2026 she has won the women's world title five times, and she is the reigning champion awaiting a match against R. Vaishali of India.[^s5][^s8]

## Early career

She learned the game at seven.[^s6] Her result at the 2004 Asian Women's Championship, at the age of 13, qualified her for the 2006 Women's World Championship. Sources disagree on her exact placing there.[^s2][^s1][^s6] She then played every women's world championship knockout from 2008 to 2017. She reached the quarter-finals in 2010 and the semi-finals in 2012, where she lost to the eventual champion Anna Ushenina.[^s1][^s2] She won the Chinese Women's Championship in 2010 and 2014.[^s2] Her third and last grandmaster norm came from second place at the Nalchik stage of the 2011–12 Women's Grand Prix.[^s2] Winning the 2015–16 Women's Grand Prix series made her the challenger for the 2018 title match.[^s2]

## World championships

In May 2018 Ju beat the champion Tan Zhongyi 5½–4½ in a ten-game match to become the seventeenth women's world champion.[^s1][^s6] Six months later she had to defend the title in a 64-player knockout, the last women's championship held in that format. She won the final against Kateryna Lagno in a tiebreak.[^s1][^s6] FIDE notes that she was only the second woman after Xie Jun to defend the title in a knockout.[^s1]

Her later defences were all matches. In January 2020 she drew 6–6 in the classical games against Aleksandra Goryachkina, then won the rapid tiebreak 2½–1½.[^s2] In July 2023 she played Lei Tingjie, with the first half in Shanghai and the second in Chongqing.[^s3][^s4] Level at 5½–5½ after eleven games, Ju won the twelfth and final game with White to keep the title 6½–5½. FIDE counted it as her fourth title, equalling Hou Yifan's record.[^s3][^s4] In April 2025 she faced Tan again and won more easily. After losing game 2, she won games 3 and 5 to 8, and a draw in game 9, a Rossolimo Sicilian, ended the match at 6½–2½ with three games unplayed.[^s6][^s5] FIDE called this her fifth title, a record among Chinese players.[^s5]

## Faster chess and team play

Ju won the Women's World Rapid Championship in 2017 and defended it in 2018.[^s1] At the end of 2024 in New York she won the Women's World Blitz Championship knockout. She beat Valentina Gunina and Vaishali on the way to an all-Chinese final against Lei. After five drawn games, Ju won a tiebreak game with White.[^s7] In Stavanger in May and June 2024 she won the first Norway Chess Women, a six-player double round robin with Armageddon games after classical draws, scoring 19 points to Anna Muzychuk's 16.[^s9] With China she won the Women's World Team Championship in 2009 and 2011 and the women's Chess Olympiad in 2016 and 2018.[^s1]

## Style and approach

Reports on her title matches stress patience and composure more than opening surprises. FIDE described her 2025 play as calm, clear and precise.[^s5] In both of her matches against Tan she held back and won by punishing her opponent's errors, as in game 6 of 2025. There Tan's 31.h4? let her break through with 31...g4.[^s5] The 2023 match showed the risk in this approach: she blundered with 18.Bxf6? in the deciding game, but Lei's later errors let her win.[^s3] For club players, her matches show how much a stable, low-error approach is worth in a long contest, more than any single brilliant game.[^s5][^s3]

## Current status

As of November 2026, FIDE lists Ju as world champion. Her challenger for the 2025–26 cycle is Vaishali, winner of the 2026 Women's Candidates. FIDE has announced a 12-game match, won by the first player to reach 6½ points, with a tiebreak if the score is level. Dates and host city had not yet been announced when this page was checked.[^s8]

## Connections

Ju is the current holder of the **Women's World Chess Championship**, the broader node for her career.[^s8] She **holds** the **Grandmaster title**, and she has **played in** the **Chess Olympiad** for China and in **Norway Chess** Women, which she won in its first edition in 2024.[^s1][^s9] Her title matches link her to **Tan Zhongyi**, the champion she dethroned and later beat again, to **Lei Tingjie**, her 2023 challenger and 2024 blitz final opponent, and to **Vaishali Rameshbabu**, her next challenger.[^s5][^s3][^s7][^s8] **Hou Yifan** is the compatriot whose record of four titles she equalled in 2023 and passed in 2025.[^s3][^s5] Her rapid and blitz titles connect her to the **World Rapid Championship** and the **World Blitz Championship**.[^s1][^s7]
