# Boris Spassky

Boris Vasilyevich Spassky was born in Leningrad (now St Petersburg) on 30 January 1937 and died in Moscow on 27 February 2025, aged 88.[^s1] He was the tenth world chess champion, holding the title from 1969 to 1972, and was inducted into the World Chess Hall of Fame in 2003.[^s1][^s3] He is best remembered as Bobby Fischer's opponent in the 1972 "Match of the Century", but his career was much broader than that one match.[^s3][^s5]

## Career

Spassky learned chess as a child evacuee from Leningrad during the Second World War, and at ten he beat Mikhail Botvinnik in a simultaneous exhibition.[^s1][^s5] He became an international master in 1953 and in 1955 won the world junior championship and the grandmaster title, at the age of 18.[^s1][^s2] A year later he made his Candidates debut at Amsterdam 1956, but then failed to qualify from the next two Interzonals and only returned to the title cycle eight years later.[^s4] Domestically he won the USSR championship outright twice, in 1961 and 1973, and lost play-offs for it twice more.[^s3][^s4]

The comeback was emphatic: in the 1965 Candidates matches he beat Paul Keres, Efim Geller and Mikhail Tal in turn.[^s2][^s4] He lost his first title match to Tigran Petrosian in Moscow in 1966 by 11½–12½, then won the 1969 rematch 12½–10½ to become champion.[^s4][^s5] In 1972 at Reykjavik he lost the crown to Fischer by 8½–12½ over 21 games; McShane credits his sportsmanship with allowing the troubled match to go ahead at all.[^s4][^s5] He remained a contender afterwards, reaching the Candidates semi-final in 1974 and the final in 1977, and in all played in seven Candidates events between 1956 and 1985.[^s3][^s4] In 1992 he and Fischer played a privately organised, unofficial rematch in Yugoslavia, which Fischer won.[^s1][^s5]

He emigrated to France in 1976, took French citizenship in 1978 and returned to Russia in 2012.[^s2][^s4] For the USSR he played seven Olympiads (1962–1978), scoring 69 points from 94 games with only one loss and collecting 13 team and individual medals; for France he played top board at three more (1984–1988).[^s2][^s4] The Hall of Fame notes that he played less often, and less strongly, after the move to France.[^s3]

## Style

Britannica singles out an adaptability it calls rarely matched in chess history.[^s1] The Hall of Fame traces a path from early, very aggressive brilliance to a mature universality in which openings, middlegames and endings were all equally strong, and FIDE describes him as no opening specialist but outstanding in complex, dynamic middlegames.[^s3][^s4] Vladimir Kramnik called him "the first truly modern universal player".[^s2] ChessBase likewise calls him a universal player with a great many attacking games to his name.[^s6]

The King's Gambit was a real weapon for him rather than a surprise: ChessBase counts 29 classical games with it in its database, with 15 wins, 14 draws and no losses, typically emerging slightly worse from the opening but outplaying opponents in the middlegame.[^s6] His first game against Fischer, at Mar del Plata 1960, was a King's Gambit win.[^s3][^s6] For a club player the lesson is that understanding the resulting middlegame matters more than the opening's theoretical verdict.

## Example: Spassky–Bronstein, Leningrad 1960

This King's Gambit from the 1960 USSR Championship is the game whose position appears in the film *From Russia with Love*.[^s3][^s5] Bronstein's ...e2 regained material with a new queen on f1, but Spassky's 16.Nxf7 and 18.Qxf5 left the black king exposed to a decisive attack.[^s5]

## Connections

Spassky sits under the **world chess championship** and **holds the title** of world champion, taken from **Tigran Petrosian** in 1969 and lost to **Bobby Fischer** in the match he **played in**, **Fischer–Spassky 1972**.[^s1][^s4] A product of the **Soviet chess school**, he **played in** seven **Candidates tournaments** and ten **Chess Olympiads** for the USSR and France.[^s2][^s3][^s4] He is closely associated with the **King's Gambit**, which his game against Bronstein **exemplifies**.[^s5][^s6]
