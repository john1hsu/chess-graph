# Emanuel Lasker

Emanuel Lasker was born on 24 December 1868 in Berlinchen, Prussia (today Barlinek in Poland), and died in New York on 11 January 1941.[^s1][^s4] He learned the game from his older brother Berthold and by the early 1890s ranked among the ten strongest players in the world.[^s2] He became the second official world champion and kept the title from 1894 to 1921, a span of 27 years that no champion has matched.[^s2][^s4]

## Winning and defending the title

Lasker challenged Wilhelm Steinitz in 1894. The match was played between 15 March and 26 May in New York, Philadelphia and Montreal, with the first player to ten wins taking the title; Lasker won ten games, lost five and drew four.[^s5] The 32-year age gap between the players remains the widest in any championship match.[^s3] A rematch in Moscow in 1896–97 ended 12½–4½ in Lasker's favour.[^s3]

Between 1907 and 1910 he defended the title repeatedly. He beat Frank Marshall 11½–3½ in 1907 and Siegbert Tarrasch 10½–5½ in 1908, winning four of the first five games against the latter.[^s3] Dawid Janowski, backed by the patron Leo Nardus, lost two matches, 8–2 in Paris and 9½–1½ in Berlin in 1910, the second being the widest margin in championship history.[^s3] The closest call came against Carl Schlechter in Vienna and Berlin in 1910: the match was tied after Lasker won the tenth and final game, and as champion he kept the title on a tie.[^s3] He finally lost in Havana in 1921 to José Raúl Capablanca, who led 9–5 when the scheduled 24-game match was stopped after 14 games.[^s3]

## Tournament record

Lasker's tournament results were as imposing as his matches. He finished only third at Hastings 1895 but then won St Petersburg 1895–96 and Nuremberg 1896, took London 1899 with 20 wins from 28 games and Paris 1900 with 14 wins from 18, shared first with Akiba Rubinstein at St Petersburg 1909, and won St Petersburg 1914 and New York 1924.[^s4] After the Nazi government seized his property in 1933 he returned to top-level play,[^s1] and in his sixties, then living in the USSR, he finished third at Moscow 1935.[^s2]

## Style and contributions

Lasker is remembered for strategic and psychological skill; the World Chess Hall of Fame notes that he brought psychology into his play and that moves his contemporaries found puzzling are now standard.[^s2] Britannica also credits him with new endgame ideas and with raising the economic standing of professionals, since he was the first master to insist on substantial fees.[^s1] He wrote widely: *Common Sense in Chess* is regarded as a classic, he published *Lasker's Chess Magazine*, and he produced *Lasker's Manual of Chess* as well as writings on philosophy and other games.[^s1][^s2]

Outside chess he was a serious mathematician. He studied in Berlin, Göttingen and Heidelberg, presented his doctoral thesis at Erlangen in 1900, and in a 1905 paper in *Mathematische Annalen* introduced primary ideals, a concept central to modern commutative algebra; he also invented the draughts-like game laska.[^s4]

## Example: St Petersburg 1914

One of his most celebrated wins came against Capablanca at St Petersburg 1914, an Exchange Ruy Lopez (ECO C68) that Lasker won as White.[^s2] After 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d4 exd4 6.Qxd4 Qxd4 7.Nxd4 Bd6 8.Nc3 Ne7 9.O-O O-O 10.f4 Re8 11.Nb3 f6 12.f5 (the position in the `fen` above), White's pawn on f5 fixes Black's kingside and cramps the light-squared bishop, while the e6 square becomes a long-term outpost for a knight; in the game score Lasker's knight reached e6 on move 16, he then advanced g4 and h4 to open lines on the kingside, and Black resigned after White's 42nd move.[^s6] The example shows the kind of concrete, unconventional decision that the Hall of Fame describes as ahead of its time.[^s2]

## Connections

Lasker sits under the **world chess championship** and **holds the title** of world champion. He is linked to **Wilhelm Steinitz**, whom he beat twice, to **José Raúl Capablanca**, who ended his reign, and to **Siegbert Tarrasch**, his 1908 challenger.[^s3] He **played in** the 1894 championship match and in Hastings 1895, and his 1914 win over Capablanca, an Exchange Variation of the **Ruy Lopez**, **exemplifies** his style.[^s2][^s5]
