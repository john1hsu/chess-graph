# Gukesh Dommaraju

Gukesh Dommaraju, usually listed as Gukesh D, is an Indian grandmaster born in Chennai on 29 May 2006 who became the eighteenth world chess champion in December 2024.[^s1][^s4] He learned the game at school at the age of seven and progressed unusually quickly through the junior ranks, later winning the title match at 18 and becoming the youngest undisputed world champion in the history of the title.[^s3][^s5]

## Junior career and the grandmaster title

Gukesh became a FIDE Master in 2015 after winning the under-9 section of the Asian School Chess Championships, won the World Under-12 Youth title, and collected several Asian youth gold medals across time controls.[^s3] He gained the International Master title at 11 in March 2018 and completed the grandmaster title on 15 January 2019 at the Delhi International GM Open, aged 12 years, 7 months and 17 days, which at the time made him the second-youngest grandmaster ever.[^s1][^s3] His coach in this period was the Indian grandmaster Vishnu Prasanna, who singled out his strategic understanding and work ethic.[^s3]

## Rise to the elite

At the 2022 Chess Olympiad in Chennai he won individual gold with 9 points from 11 games, helping India to a team bronze, and he crossed 2700 the same year.[^s3] In August 2023 he became the youngest player ever to pass a 2750 rating, and in September 2023 he overtook Viswanathan Anand as India's highest-rated player, ending Anand's 37-year run at the top of the national list.[^s1][^s3] A second place in the 2023 FIDE Circuit qualified him for the 2024 Candidates Tournament.[^s3]

## Candidates, Olympiad and world title (2024)

Seeded only sixth by rating in the eight-player Candidates in Toronto in April 2024, Gukesh finished clear first with 9/14 and became the youngest Candidates winner, at 17 years, 10 months and 24 days.[^s1][^s2] Later that year, at the Budapest Olympiad, he scored 9/10 on board one (eight wins and two draws), took individual gold and helped India to its first open-section team gold.[^s1][^s2]

The title match against Ding Liren in Singapore (25 November–13 December 2024) was over 14 classical games, first to 7.5 points.[^s1] Gukesh lost the first game, won the third and went ahead by winning game 11; Ding levelled in game 12, game 13 was drawn, and in the final classical game Ding blundered in a rook-and-bishop ending, allowing a rook trade into a lost king-and-pawn ending.[^s1][^s4] Gukesh won 7.5–6.5.[^s4] The FIDE museum describes his play as built on accurate calculation, few mistakes and notable composure under pressure.[^s2]

## As champion (2025–2026)

In June 2025, in round six of Norway Chess, Gukesh recorded his first classical win over Magnus Carlsen, turning around a position in which Carlsen had been clearly winning before losing control in time trouble.[^s8] Commentators have since described his tournament results as inconsistent: on the November 2026 FIDE list he is rated 2710 and ranked 26th in the world, 72 points below his challenger Javokhir Sindarov (2782, world No. 4).[^s7][^s9]

His first defence, against Sindarov, the winner of the 2025 World Cup and the 2026 Candidates in Cyprus, is scheduled for 22 November–12 December 2026 at the Fondation Martin Bodmer in Cologny, Geneva, again over a maximum of 14 classical games with rapid and blitz tiebreaks if the score is 7–7 (as of November 2026; the result was not known when this article was verified).[^s5][^s9]

## Example: Ding–Gukesh, game 14, 2024

The title-deciding game, a Zukertort Opening with a reversed Grünfeld set-up (ECO A08), shows Gukesh's patience: with Black he kept pressing in the ending for over 50 moves until 55.Rf2 allowed 55...Rxf2 56.Kxf2 Bd5, after which the bishop trade left a winning pawn ending.[^s4][^s6]

## Connections

Gukesh sits under the World Chess Championship as the eighteenth holder of the title, succeeding Ding Liren, and his route to the match ran through the Candidates Tournament, which he won in 2024 and which produced his 2026 challenger, Javokhir Sindarov.[^s2][^s5] He played in two Chess Olympiads with individual gold on each occasion, holds the grandmaster title, and replaced Viswanathan Anand as India's top-rated player in 2023.[^s1][^s3] The deciding game of his title match, and his 2025 classical win over Magnus Carlsen, are the games most often cited from his career so far.[^s4][^s8]
