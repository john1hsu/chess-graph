# Magnus Carlsen

Sven Magnus Øen Carlsen is a Norwegian grandmaster, born in Tønsberg on 30 November 1990, who held the world championship from 2013 until he chose not to defend it, and who has been the world's top-rated player without interruption since July 2011.[^s1][^s2] His father taught him the moves at five; FIDE's 2013 match profile notes that he showed little interest at first before returning to the game and rising unusually fast.[^s3]

## Prodigy years

Carlsen earned all three International Master norms in under eight months in 2003, before turning 13.[^s3] He won the C group at Wijk aan Zee in January 2004 with 10½/13, and on 26 April 2004 became a grandmaster at 13 years and 148 days, then the second-youngest in history.[^s3] In 2005 he qualified through the World Cup for the Candidates cycle as its youngest participant, and in 2007 he won Biel and reached the World Cup semi-finals.[^s3] In November 2009 he crossed 2800 at 18 years and 336 days, the youngest player to do so, and on 1 January 2010 he became the youngest world number one ever.[^s3][^s2] In January 2013 his rating of 2861 broke Garry Kasparov's record of 2851; his peak of 2882 remains the highest in history.[^s3][^s2]

## World champion

Carlsen withdrew from the 2010 Candidates, unhappy with its format, then won the 2013 Candidates in London on tie-break over Vladimir Kramnik.[^s2] He took the title from Viswanathan Anand in Chennai in 2013 by 6½–3½, at that time the second-youngest champion.[^s2][^s1] He then won four defences: against Anand in Sochi in 2014 (6½–4½), Sergey Karjakin in New York in 2016 and Fabiano Caruana in London in 2018 (both decided in rapid tie-breaks), and Ian Nepomniachtchi in Dubai in 2021 (7½–3½).[^s2] After the 2021 match he announced he would not defend again, a decision Britannica dates to 2022, and he later withdrew formally, ending a reign of almost ten years.[^s1][^s2]

## Rapid, blitz and the World Cup

Carlsen has dominated the faster formats. In 2019 he became the first player to hold the classical, rapid and blitz world titles at the same time.[^s1] At the 2025 World Rapid and Blitz Championships in Doha he won the rapid with 10½/13, a point clear, for his sixth rapid title (after 2014, 2015, 2019, 2022 and 2023), and then the blitz, beating Nodirbek Abdusattorov 2½–1½ in the knockout final for his ninth blitz title.[^s4][^s5] He also won the 2023 World Cup, and in 2024 received a FIDE Best Player award marking the federation's centenary.[^s2]

## Style

Players quoted by FIDE describe a universal player rather than an opening specialist. Kasparov likened him to a blend of Fischer and Karpov and stressed his tenacity in holding positions; Anand pointed to his physical fitness and willingness to play every game to the end; his long-time second Peter Heine Nielsen linked his intuition to huge numbers of fast online games combined with study of the classics.[^s2] Carlsen's own summary of his attitude is that a player has to be merciless.[^s2] Anand's point about playing every game to the end is the practical lesson for club players: positions that look level often still hold chances for the side that keeps asking questions.[^s2]

## Current status (as of November 2026)

On the November 2026 FIDE list Carlsen is rated 2823 and ranked first, 31 points ahead of Hikaru Nakamura.[^s6] His lead has narrowed during 2026: at the Total Chess World Championship Tour pilot in Budapest (10–21 November 2026), a loss to Haik Martirosyan left him 7.2 points ahead of Fabiano Caruana on the live list on 13 November, with his fifteen-year run at number one under threat.[^s7]

## Example: Carlsen–Anand, Sochi 2014, game 6

This Kan Sicilian (ECO B41) shows Carlsen's taste for queenless middlegames with a small, lasting edge.[^s8] White exchanged queens on move 9 and built pressure against Black's king, stuck on c7 and then b7. After 26.Kd2? Anand could have won material with 26...Nxe5, but replied 26...a4 and lost a pawn-down ending after White opened the kingside.[^s8]

## Connections

Carlsen sits under the World Chess Championship as the champion who succeeded Viswanathan Anand in 2013 and gave up the title after his fifth match, and he remains the rating leader above the current generation of title contenders such as Gukesh Dommaraju and Javokhir Sindarov.[^s2][^s6] He reached the title through the Candidates Tournament, has won the FIDE World Cup, and holds the grandmaster title.[^s2][^s3] His 2882 peak and long reign at the top of the list make him the central modern figure for the Elo rating system, and Kasparov, whose rating record he broke, is his closest historical comparison.[^s2][^s3] The 2014 game 6 above is a well-known example of a tactical chance missed by both sides at world-championship level.[^s8]
