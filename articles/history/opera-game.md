# Opera Game

The **Opera Game** is the casual game Paul Morphy won in Paris in 1858 against two amateurs consulting together, Duke Karl II of Brunswick and Count Isouard (also spelled Isoard).[^s1][^s4] Tradition places it in the Duke's private box at the Italian Opera during a performance, which gave the game its name.[^s1][^s4] In just 17 moves Morphy developed every piece, opened lines towards the uncastled black king and finished with a queen sacrifice and a mate by his last rook. Frank Marshall called it the most famous game of all time, and it remains a fixture of beginners' books.[^s1][^s5]

## Circumstances and record

Morphy came to Europe in 1858 after winning the First American Chess Congress in 1857, and in that year he beat the leading European masters, including Adolf Anderssen. Without any formal title he came to be regarded as the world's best player.[^s2][^s3] His opponents here were far weaker. The Duke, a strong amateur, had ruled the Duchy of Brunswick from 1823 until a revolt in 1830 drove him into exile; little is known about the Count.[^s4]

Almost everything beyond the moves is disputed. David Lawson's 1976 biography dates the game 2 November 1858 during *The Barber of Seville*, and ChessBase repeats that date.[^s1][^s4] However, Fabrizio Zavatarelli found no theatre performances listed in *La Presse* for 2 November and suggested 4 November, or one of the October performances of *Norma*.[^s1][^s5] Max Lange's 1860 book on Morphy describes the game as played at the Italian Opera during *The Barber of Seville*, while his later German edition heads it October 1858, and the two cannot both be right.[^s1] Other writers name *Norma* or *The Marriage of Figaro*. Edward Winter, who has collected the evidence, declines to say which opera was being performed.[^s1][^s5]

## How the game went

The opening is a **Philidor Defence** (2...d6), and Black's 3...Bg4 pins the knight but then gives up the bishop pair after 4.dxe5 Bxf3 5.Qxf3, leaving White ahead in development with the queen already active.[^s1][^s4] After 6.Bc4 and 7.Qb3, White hits f7 and b7 at once; Black's 7...Qe7 guards f7 but blocks the f8-bishop and leaves the king in the centre.[^s1] Emanuel Lasker remarked that grabbing material on move 8 would have been a butcher's method rather than an artist's, and Morphy instead kept developing with 8.Nc3 and 9.Bg5.[^s1]

From move 10 Morphy turned his lead in development into an attack. He gave a knight for two pawns with 10.Nxb5 cxb5 11.Bxb5+, pinning the d7-knight, then castled long so that his rook joined the d-file with tempo.[^s1] The exchange sacrifice 13.Rxd7 removed a defender, and 14.Rd1 brought the second rook onto the pinned file. Then came the famous finish: 16.Qb8+ forces 16...Nxb8, removing the last guard of d8, and 17.Rd8 is mate because the black queen and bishop block the king's escape squares.[^s1][^s4]

## Reputation and lessons

ChessBase sums up the game's appeal: almost every white move develops a piece, makes a threat or captures something, the pattern of Morphy's play in general.[^s4] Britannica and the World Chess Hall of Fame credit Morphy with showing the value of development before attack, which this game shows in miniature.[^s2][^s3] Not everyone admires it. John Nunn thought it not an especially good game and questioned its place in beginners' books, while Yasser Seirawan argued it is the most quoted game of all.[^s1][^s5] Bobby Fischer also presented it on television.[^s4]

For a club player the practical points are clear. Black's early bishop trade and queen move cost time, his king stayed in the centre, and his pieces on the d-file ended up pinned against it. Once White's rooks reached that open file, the material he had given up no longer mattered.[^s1][^s4]

## Connections

The Opera Game is a classic product of the **Romantic era** and was **played by** Paul Morphy, whose games bridged that era and the positional approach that followed.[^s2][^s3] It **exemplifies** rapid **development** and the **sacrifice** for an attack. It is related to the **Philidor Defence**, its opening, to the **pin**, which decides the game on the d-file, to the **initiative**, and to the **back-rank mate**, which its final position closely resembles.[^s1][^s4]
