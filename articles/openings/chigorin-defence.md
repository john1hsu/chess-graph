# Chigorin Defence

The Chigorin Defence is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 Nc6, filed under ECO D07.[^s7] Black ignores the classical advice not to block the c-pawn: the knight on c6 hits d4 at once, supports the freeing advance ...e5 and keeps ...dxc4 in reserve, at the cost of giving up the usual ...c5 or ...c6 counter-play against White's centre.[^s4] The resulting positions are full of strategic imbalances, and Valery Bronznik's monograph presents the defence as a weapon for aggressive players, since the early attack on White's centre gives White many chances to go wrong.[^s2]

## History

The move was played long before Chigorin; Chessgames.com lists a von der Goltz–von der Lasa game from Berlin 1837.[^s7] GM Bryan Smith argues that Mikhail Chigorin was nevertheless the first to adopt it as a conscious system.[^s3] His first three games with 2...Nc6 were all against Harry Nelson Pillsbury in the St Petersburg quadrangular tournament of 1895–96: he lost the first badly, came back with new ideas in the second, and drew the third after Pillsbury changed his approach.[^s3] Before that, Chigorin had used a related idea, an early ...Bg4 against Nf3, in his 1889 match with Steinitz and in his Hastings 1895 win over Lasker.[^s3]

For most of the twentieth century the defence had a dubious reputation.[^s1] Its rehabilitation is credited to Alexander Morozevich and to Igor Miladinovic, together with Bronznik's book, first published in German in 2001.[^s1][^s2] Miladinovic is the most frequent practitioner in the Chessgames.com database, with 73 games.[^s7] Davide Nastasio counted 35 Morozevich games with the line between 1994 and 2015, scoring twelve wins, twelve losses and eleven draws; against the very top players he mostly drew or lost.[^s5] Later users include Richard Rapport, and Vassily Ivanchuk once beat Ding Liren with it.[^s5]

## Main lines and ideas

A recurring theme is that Black trades a bishop for a knight: the light-squared bishop for the knight on f3 and often the dark-squared bishop for the knight on c3, playing with a knight pair and quick development rather than material or the two bishops.[^s5] Smith compares the resulting fights over light squares and White's doubled pawns with Nimzo-Indian themes.[^s3]

- **3.cxd5 Qxd5**: after 4.e3 e5 5.Nc3 Bb4 6.Bd2 Bxc3 7.Bxc3 exd4 8.Ne2, IM Hannes Langrock notes that the natural 8...Bg4 9.f3 works poorly, and that 8...Nf6 9.Nxd4 O-O 10.Nb5 Bg4 leaves Black fine with best play.[^s4]
- **3.Nc3**: Black can answer 3...Nf6 4.cxd5 Nxd5 5.Nf3 e5 6.dxe5 Bb4, and after 7.Bd2 Nxc3 8.bxc3 Ba5 the weak e5-pawn gives Black enough compensation.[^s4] Another try is 3...dxc4 4.Nf3 Nf6 5.Bg5, which IM John Watson highlighted as Boris Avrukh's recommendation for White.[^s6]
- **3.Nf3**: the critical test in GM Fabien Libiszewski's 2026 course is 3...Bg4 4.cxd5 Bxf3 5.gxf3 Qxd5 6.e3 e5 7.Nc3 Bb4 8.Bd2 Bxc3 9.bxc3, where engines prefer White's bishop pair and central pawns but Black keeps practical chances.[^s4] Engines favour 3...e6 instead, though it gives up the thematic ...Bg4 and ...e5.[^s4]

## Pitfalls and evaluation

Objectively White is better in the critical lines; Black's chances are practical, coming from unclear king safety and tactical complications.[^s4] Langrock therefore suggests the defence as a surprise weapon, especially at faster time controls, rather than a main classical system, and Nastasio observed that no top-ten player was using it.[^s4][^s5] Database results agree: White scores about 45 per cent wins to Black's 26 per cent in Chessgames.com's D07 games.[^s7] If Black drifts into passivity, the trade of bishops for knights simply leaves White with the bishop pair and a strong centre.

## Example

The critical 3.Nf3 line from Libiszewski's course:[^s4]

```pgn
[Event "Chigorin Defence, 3.Nf3 Bg4"]
[Result "*"]

1. d4 d5 2. c4 Nc6 3. Nf3 Bg4 4. cxd5 Bxf3 5. gxf3 Qxd5 6. e3 e5 7. Nc3 Bb4 8. Bd2 Bxc3 9. bxc3 *
```

## Connections

The Chigorin is a branch of the **Queen's Gambit** and, like the **Albin Countergambit** that Watson reviewed alongside it, a sharp **alternative to** the solid **Queen's Gambit Declined**.[^s6] It is **named after** **Mikhail Chigorin** and was **championed** in modern practice by **Alexander Morozevich**.[^s3][^s1] Its middlegames turn on **knight versus bishop** and on whether White's **bishop pair** comes to life, themes it shares with the **Nimzo-Indian Defence**.[^s3][^s5]
