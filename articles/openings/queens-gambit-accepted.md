# Queen's Gambit Accepted

The Queen's Gambit Accepted (QGA) arises after 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4: instead of supporting d5, Black takes the offered pawn.[^s5] Black rarely tries to keep it. The point of the capture is to clear the centre so that Black's pieces develop freely, with counterplay against White's d4-pawn, while White in return gets two centre pawns against one and can usually regain c4 at once with the f1-bishop.[^s5] It is one of the oldest defences to 1.d4: grandmaster Max Dlugy, who has played it for about forty years, traces lines back to 1795 and points to Alexander McDonnell's use of it in his 1834 match against Louis-Charles Mahé de La Bourdonnais.[^s2] Chessgames.com files the opening under ECO D20 and holds games with it from across the history of the game.[^s6]

## Main ideas

Black's trade-off is simple: surrendering the d5 strongpoint gives White a potential pawn centre, but Black gains time and open lines for the pieces.[^s5] Dlugy describes Black's typical aims as fighting for the c4-pawn, using d5 as a blockade square for a knight or the light-squared bishop, and accepting structural concessions, such as letting White double Black's f-pawns, in return for active piece play.[^s2] His foreword-writer, Alex Fishbein, stresses the flip side: if White does not regain the c4-pawn early enough, speculative sacrifices may simply leave White a pawn down without compensation.[^s1]

## Main branches

- **Classical, 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4 c5 6.0-0 a6.** White recovers the pawn calmly and Black strikes at d4 with ...c5. Chessgames.com files this position as the Classical main line, ECO D27, and its sample of several thousand games shows about half of them drawn.[^s7] Grandmaster Damian Lemos notes that in this main line Black often exchanges on d4 with ...cxd4, which leaves White with an isolated queen's pawn.[^s3] Dlugy also covers White's plan of a2–a4 to restrain ...b5 (which he files as the Rubinstein Variation) and the early 4.Nc3.[^s2]
- **Central Variation, 3.e4.** White takes the whole centre at once. Lars Schandorff calls it White's space-gaining choice but admits the QGA is a tough opening to meet, since Black has several ways to challenge White's centre: 3...b5, 3...c5, 3...e5, 3...Nc6 and 3...Nf6 all get chapters in his book.[^s4] Lemos chooses 3.e4 for White as more challenging than the main line, partly because lines such as 3...Nf6 4.e5 give White extra space.[^s3] Dlugy's repertoire for Black meets it with 3...e5 4.Nf3 Bb4+, splitting into 5.Nbd2, 5.Bd2 and 5.Nc3.[^s2]
- **Quiet Line, 3.e3.** Dlugy recommends 3...e5 here too; after 4.Bxc4 exd4 5.exd4 he prefers 5...Bd6 to 5...Be7, which he says allows White ideas such as f4 and Ne5.[^s2]

## Pitfalls

The classic mistake is trying to keep the extra pawn with ...b5. Lemos calls 3.e4 b5 risky for Black: after 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5 6.Nc3 White attacks the queenside pawns, and he regards 6...Bd7 as one of Black's few playable options.[^s3] Dlugy nevertheless includes 3...b5 in his repertoire, naming it the Zaltsman Variation after his coach, and credits it with reviving interest in the opening at top level.[^s1] Black must also guard against falling behind in development: Lemos stresses that a Black king left in the centre is a typical target and that White's d4–d5 break and control of e5 are key themes.[^s3] In the Quiet Line, Dlugy calls an early Qb3 dubious, as the queen is misplaced there.[^s2]

## Example

After 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4 c5 6.0-0 a6, White has regained the pawn and leads in development, while Black prepares ...b5 and ...Bb7 to contest the long diagonal and can release the central tension with ...cxd4.[^s7][^s3] If White answers ...cxd4 with exd4, the familiar isolated-pawn middlegame arises, with White playing for piece activity and Black for a blockade on d5.[^s3][^s2]

## Connections

The QGA is the 2...dxc4 branch of the **Queen's Gambit** and an **alternative to** the **Queen's Gambit Declined** (2...e6) and the **Slav Defence** (2...c6), the other two main replies.[^s5] Its classical main line typically **leads to** an **isolated queen's pawn** for White after ...cxd4.[^s3] As a 1.d4 d5 2.c4 opening it belongs to the family of **closed games**, although the early capture on c4 gives it more open, dynamic positions than the Declined.[^s5][^s4]
