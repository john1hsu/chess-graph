# Sicilian Defence

The Sicilian Defence is Black's reply 1...c5 to 1.e4. ChessBase Learn describes it as the most popular answer to the king's pawn and the opening most often played by grandmasters and amateurs alike.[^s1] Britannica presents it as the most aggressive of Black's options, one that gives up control of the centre for the time being.[^s2] The opening position 1.e4 c5 carries the ECO code B20, and a game database page for that code holds games stretching back to the seventeenth century.[^s6]

## History

The Sicilian was slow to win respect. Edward Winter collects nineteenth- and early twentieth-century verdicts against it: in 1892 James Mason wrote that Henry Bird was nearly the only strong player who still believed in it, and as late as 1906 and 1914 opening manuals marked 1...c5 with a question mark.[^s3] Its theory grew from players such as Louis Paulsen, whom the authors of *Dismantling the Sicilian* call the real father of the defence.[^s4] Britannica credits the Soviet school, and Isaak Boleslavsky in particular, with showing that Black's concession in the centre could be balanced by active counterplay, for example with the pawn set-up ...d6 and ...e5 despite the hole it leaves on d5.[^s2] Winter's page also traces the names of many sub-variations, such as the Dragon, whose printed name he dates to the 1920s.[^s3]

## Main ideas

After 1...c5 the c-pawn controls d4 without blocking any black piece. In the **Open Sicilian**, 2.Nf3 followed by 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4, White opens the centre and gains a lead in development and space, while Black obtains an asymmetrical structure, and the half-open c-file for its rooks.[^s1] Black's counterplay usually comes from the c-file and from queenside expansion with ...a6 and ...b5; White's typical answers are pressure down the d-file and a kingside pawn attack, very often after castling queenside.[^s1] Ivan Saric's 2023 repertoire for White is built around the English Attack set-up of Be3, f3, Qd2 and 0-0-0, followed by g4 and h4, which he calls simple and effective.[^s5]

## Main variations

The key branching point is the main line 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3, where 4...Nf6 hits e4 and 5.Nc3 defends it.[^s1]

- **Najdorf, 5...a6.** Black prepares ...b5 and keeps options open; White chooses among 6.Be3, 6.Bg5 and 6.Be2. ChessBase Learn calls Garry Kasparov the line's greatest exponent.[^s1]
- **Dragon, 5...g6.** Black fianchettoes on g7 and castles short; White usually answers with Be3, f3, Qd2 and long castling, for example 6.Be3 Bg7 7.f3 Nc6 8.Qd2 0-0 9.0-0-0, and both sides race on opposite wings.[^s1][^s5]
- **Classical and Richter-Rauzer, 5...Nc6 6.Bg5.** Black develops naturally; White aims for long castling, and Bxf6 can leave Black with doubled f-pawns.[^s1]
- **Scheveningen, 5...e6.** A flexible small centre, met by the sharp Keres Attack 6.g4 or a quieter 6.Be2.[^s1]
- **Lines with 2...e6 and 2...Nc6.** These include the Taimanov (2...e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7), the Paulsen (4...a6), the Sveshnikov (2...Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 e5) and the Accelerated Dragon (4...g6), where 5.c4 sets up the Maróczy Bind.[^s1][^s4]
- **Anti-Sicilians.** White can avoid the open lines with the Closed Sicilian (2.Nc3 Nc6 3.g3 g6), the Alapin 2.c3, which prepares d4 so that White can recapture with a pawn, or the Rossolimo 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5.[^s1]

## Plans and pitfalls

Many Sicilian structures trade safety for imbalance. In the Sveshnikov, after 6.Ndb5 d6 7.Bg5 a6 8.Na3 b5, Black gets activity and space but leaves the d5-square permanently weak, a hole White aims to occupy with a knight.[^s1] In the Taimanov and Four Knights, Black must watch the d6-square once a white knight reaches b5.[^s1] For White, the Open Sicilian demands concrete knowledge: *Dismantling the Sicilian* argues that queenside castling is logical in these positions and that the f3 set-up gives a solid e4-pawn and a clear plan, but every black system calls for a specific answer.[^s4]

## Connections

The Sicilian is a **semi-open game** and the main **alternative to the Caro-Kann Defence** and the **French Defence** as Black's replies to 1.e4 other than 1...e5.[^s1] It **leads to** the **Najdorf**, **Dragon** and **Sveshnikov Variations**, each a body of theory in its own right, and is closely associated with **Garry Kasparov**.[^s1] Its rise owes much to the **Soviet chess school**, and the Sveshnikov's d5-square is a textbook **outpost**.[^s2][^s1]
