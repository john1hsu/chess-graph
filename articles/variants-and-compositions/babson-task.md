# Babson task

The Babson task is one of the most famous challenges in chess composition. After White's key move, Black can promote a pawn to a queen, a rook, a bishop or a knight, and in each of these four variations White's only good reply is to promote a pawn to exactly the same piece.[^s2][^s5] In its strict form the black promotions all happen with one pawn on one square, and the white answers with one pawn on one square, so the solution shows a Black **Allumwandlung** mirrored, piece for piece, by a White one.[^s4] John Nunn calls it a task that needs four matched black and white promotions.[^s1]

## Origin

The task carries the name of the American problemist Joseph Ney Babson, who died in 1929 without seeing a direct-mate solution.[^s2] Accounts usually date his experiments with the idea to about 1925.[^s2][^s5] Peter Hoffmann traces it further back, to a call for a Babson competition that appeared in the *Pittsburgh Gazette Times* on 1 June 1913.[^s6] In freer genres the task fell quickly: Nunn notes that selfmate Babsons were achieved in the 1920s, while the direct mate resisted composers for over half a century.[^s1] Around 1930 André Chéron predicted that a direct-mate version would never be composed.[^s2]

## Why the direct mate is so hard

In a direct mate White must force mate in a fixed number of moves against every defence, so each black promotion has to be a real defence and each matching white promotion has to be the only way through. A white queen is always the most powerful choice, so the composer needs four separate reasons why it fails: typically a black defence that would leave Black stalemated if White had a queen, or one that breaks the mating net in a way only the matching piece can repair.[^s4][^s5] The knight is the worst offender because its short reach makes a forced knight answer hard to motivate.[^s2] The French composer Pierre Drumare spent about four hours a day on the problem from 1960; his attempts produced an illegal position with nine rooks and nine bishops, and in 1982, after 22 years, he gave up.[^s2][^s4]

## Yarosh's solution (1983)

In 1983 Leonid Yarosh, a 26-year-old football and chess trainer from Kazan, solved the direct mate.[^s2][^s4] Krabbé lists his first version, a mate in four published in *Shakhmaty v SSSR* in March 1983, as the first direct-mate Babson; its key captured a black knight, which purists count as a flaw.[^s3] A lighter version later that year, with the quiet key 1.a7!, took first prize. In it Black's pawn takes on b1 and promotes, White's pawn takes on b8 and copies the promotion, and in each line a queen would fail to a specific defence such as a stalemate trick or a knight capture on d2.[^s2][^s4] Its bishop line still allowed two different mates on move four, and a third version, dedicated to Drumare, removed that dual.[^s2][^s3] Nunn writes that the solution brought the previously unknown Soviet composer instant fame.[^s1]

## After 1983

The breakthrough triggered a run of new settings: Krabbé's chronology runs through G. Sacharov (1984), further Drumare and Yarosh settings, many problems by Peter Hoffmann from 1985 on, and Hoffmann's 2005 problem described as the first cyclic Babson.[^s3] Friedel counted about 30 echo-Babsons by 2023, eight of them with dual-free main lines, the first being Hoffmann's of 1986.[^s4] No setting yet has dual-free side lines as well, and Werner Keym offered 100 euros in 2010 for the first one.[^s4] Endgame study composers took up the challenge separately, building three-quarter versions from the late 1970s onward.[^s6] In 2024 Gady Costeff published a study with all four matched promotions, which Luke McShane compared to the first four-minute mile.[^s5][^s6]

## Connections

The Babson task is the strictest form of the **Allumwandlung**, one of the classic tasks of the **chess problem**.[^s1][^s4] It is named after **Joseph Ney Babson** and its first direct-mate realisation is credited to **Leonid Yarosh**.[^s2][^s3] It rests on **pawn promotion** and is driven by **underpromotion**, with **stalemate** avoidance as a common motive for the minor promotions.[^s4][^s5] It was achieved early in the **selfmate** and has since been extended to the **endgame study**.[^s1][^s6]
